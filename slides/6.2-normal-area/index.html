Chance is a share

The shaded part is a share of everyone.
1 means 100%. Half the bell is 0.50.

Say: this is the first time the word probability means something in this course. It is the shaded share of the bell. The whole bell is 1, which is 100%. Half is 0.50, which is 50%. A probability is never bigger than 1. Move your hand over the shaded side and call that the chance a value lands there. A percentile is the Unit 2 word: a cutoff with a given share of the people on one side. Desmos, not the book’s TI buttons. cdf asks for an area. inversecdf asks for the cutoff. Less-than and less-than-or-equal are the same area on a smooth curve. Say that only if someone asks. About 30 minutes. If you are short, stop after the golf scores. The phone ages can wait. Example 6.7 · read a decimal as a percent

The two shares add to 100%

0.0228 is 2.28%. The rest is 1 − 0.0228 = 0.9772, which is 97.72%. They add to 100%. Try It 6.7 is the same picture: 0.012 is 1.2%, so the right share is 0.988, or 98.8%.

Say: a probability in this chapter is a decimal from 0 to 1. To hear it as a percent, move the decimal two places. 0.0228 is 2.28%, a small chance. 0.9772 is 97.72%, almost certain. The line in the picture is z = −2, because that is the table area. The book does not name an x for this one. Have them add 0.0228 + 0.9772 and see 1. That is the whole bell. Try It 6.7 is the same move: 0.012 is 1.2% on the left, so the right side is 0.988, or 98.8%. The book writes P(X < x) for the left share. Read it as 'the chance a value is under x.' Do not stop on the symbol.

Example 6.8 · final exams, mean 63, step 5

The chance of a score over 65

(65 − 63) / 5 = 0.4. The left share is 0.6554, and 1 − 0.6554 = 0.3446, about one student in three. cdf is the left share, so subtract it from 1.

DESMOS  1 - normaldist(63,5).cdf(65)

Say: translate before you admire the decimal. 0.3446 is about 34.5%, about one student in three. A score over 65 is ordinary, because 65 is less than one step above the average of 63. (65−63)/5 = 0.4. The book’s table area to the left of that is 0.6554, and 1 − 0.6554 = 0.3446. Type the Desmos line yourself. Tell them cdf means the left share, so the 1 minus is the question they were asked. They do not need the z-score to get the area. The z-score only explains why the chance is not small. Watch for: students who report 0.6554. That is the share who scored 65 or under.

Example 6.8 · the same exams

A sure thing, and two cutoffs

A percentile is a cutoff, not a chance. 85 is about four steps above 63, so the share under 85 is about 100%. The book rounds it to 1.

DESMOS  normaldist(63,5).inversecdf(0.90)

Say: under 85 is not a serious question. Four steps above the average catches essentially everyone, so the chance is about 100%, and the book rounds the area to 1. A percentile is not a chance. It is a cutoff. The 90th percentile, 69.4, means 90 out of 100 scores are that or lower. Same word as Unit 2. Desmos: normaldist(63,5).inversecdf(0.90). You hand it the share 0.90 and it hands back the score. The book’s TI command is invNorm(0.90, 63, 5). Do not teach the buttons. The 70th percentile is 65.6. One sentence in the book says 65.5. The calculator line says 65.6. Use 65.6. Watch for: students who think 69.4 is a probability. Ask: is 69.4 between 0 and 1? No. It is a score.

Your turn · Try It 6.8 · 3 minutes

The chance of a score under 65

The average golf score is 68. One step is 3. What share of golfers score under 65?

65 is one step below 68. Write the share to the left of 65 as a decimal.

DESMOS  normaldist(68,3).cdf(65)

Try It 6.8. The book does not print this answer. Give them the Desmos line and three minutes. A strong student sees z = (65−68)/3 = −1 before they type anything: one step below the mean, so the left tail outside the middle 68% is about 16%. The precise share is 0.1587. If they report 0.84, they found the share who scored over 65.

Example 6.11 · smartphone users, ages 13 to 55+

Average age 36.9, step 13.9

Q1 is 27.5246 years. Q3 is 46.2754. The IQR is 18.8 years. Forty percent are 40.4 or older, so hand Desmos the left share 0.60.

DESMOS  normaldist(36.9,13.9).inversecdf(0.60)

This slide can wait if the hour is gone. Say: these are the quartiles from Unit 2, found on a bell instead of a list. The book prints Q1 = 27.5246 and Q3 = 46.2754. Subtract: 46.2754 − 27.5246 = 18.75, and the book rounds the IQR to 18.8 years.
