A range for the mean

The sample mean is the point estimate.
The margin of error is the reach on each side.

SECTION 8.1

Prof. Rebin Muhammad

Montgomery College

Say: a confidence interval is a range for the population mean. The sample mean sits in the middle. That middle number is the point estimate. The margin of error is how far the interval reaches on each side. Point at 7, then at 4.5 and 9.5. 7 minus 2.5 is 4.5. 7 plus 2.5 is 9.5. This section is for a mean when the population standard deviation is known. Section 8.2 is the case when it is not. The likely mistake is reading 4.5 to 9.5 as a range that holds most of the data. It is a range for the mean. About one class period. Unit 5 homework, the test, and the discussion are due Wednesday, November 19.

The words, then the symbols

Point estimate, standard error, margin of error

$$\mathrm{EBM} = z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$$

x̄ is the sample mean, the point estimate for μ, the population mean. σ is the population standard deviation. In this section it is known. n is the sample size.

The standard error is σ over the square root of n. It is the spread of the sample mean. z is the standard-normal cutoff with the confidence level in the middle. EBM is the error bound, the margin of error.

The interval is x̄ minus EBM to x̄ plus EBM. To recover the sample mean, average the two ends.

Say the names before the formula. x-bar is the sample mean. It estimates mu, the population mean. Sigma is the population standard deviation, and in this section it is known. The standard error is sigma divided by the square root of n. Students met that spread in section 7.1. z is how many standard errors you travel. For 90%, each tail is 5%, and the book uses z = 1.645. Positive is to the right of 0. The same distance is used on both sides of the sample mean. EBM is the error bound for the mean, the margin of error. To build the interval, start at the sample mean and move that far both ways. To go backwards, average the two ends. Example 8.6: (67.18 + 68.82) / 2 = 68, and 68.82 minus 68 = 0.82. The likely mistake is calling z a score on the exam. z is a count of standard errors. Alpha is 1 minus the confidence level. Say that only if someone asks.

Example 8.1

Mean 7, error bound 2.5

We estimate with 95% confidence that the true population mean is between 4.5 and 9.5.

4.5 and 9.5 are ends of a range for the mean. They are not data values.

Point at 7. That is the sample mean, the point estimate. The error bound is 2.5, so the interval reaches 2.5 below 7 and 2.5 above 7. 7 minus 2.5 is 4.5. 7 plus 2.5 is 9.5. Read the book’s sentence once: we estimate with 95% confidence that the true population mean is between 4.5 and 9.5. The likely mistake is hearing 4.5 to 9.5 as the scores people got. Those ends were built from the mean and the error bound.

Example 8.2 · statistics exams

Mean 68, standard deviation 3, n = 36

68 − 0.8225 = 67.18 and 68 + 0.8225 = 68.82. The calculator prints (67.178, 68.822).

We estimate with 90% confidence that the true mean exam score is between 67.18 and 68.82.

DESMOS ↗  1.645*3/sqrt(36)  then 68 minus that, and 68 plus that

The population standard deviation is 3 points. The sample size is 36. The sample mean is 68. The confidence level is 90%, so each tail is 5%, and the book uses z = 1.645. The standard error is 3 divided by the square root of 36. The square root of 36 is 6, so the standard error is 0.5. The error bound is 1.645 times 0.5, which is 0.8225. 68 minus 0.8225 is 67.1775, and the book rounds to 67.18. 68 plus 0.8225 is 68.8225, and the book rounds to 68.82. The calculator prints (67.178, 68.822). Read the sentence: we estimate with 90% confidence that the true mean exam score is between 67.18 and 68.82. The likely mistake is using 3 as the standard error and getting 68 plus or minus 1.645 times 3. The 3 has to be divided by the square root of n.

What 90% means

About 90 of 100 intervals, not 90% of the scores

THE BOOK’S SENTENCE

Ninety percent of all confidence intervals constructed in this way contain the true mean exam score.

If we built 100 intervals this way, we would expect 90 of them to contain the true mean.

NOT THIS

67.18 to 68.82 is not the range where 90% of students scored.

It is a range for the mean. This one interval either contains the mean or it does not.

Read the book’s sentence. Ninety percent of intervals made this way contain the true mean. The picture in the book is: build 100 of them, and expect 90 to catch the mean. Do not say there is a 90% chance that this mean is between 67.18 and 68.82. The mean is a fixed number. The interval is what moved. The other mistake is treating 67.18 to 68.82 as the scores of 90% of the students. One standard deviation of the scores is 3 points, so the scores themselves sit much farther from 68 than this interval does.

Example 8.4 · the same exams

95% reaches farther than 90%

1.96 × 0.5 = 0.98. Then 68 − 0.98 = 67.02 and 68 + 0.98 = 68.98.

A higher confidence level makes a wider interval.

Same sample mean, same standard deviation, same n. Only the confidence level changes. For 95%, alpha is 0.05, so each tail is 0.025, and the book uses z = 1.96. The standard error is still 0.5. The error bound is 1.96 times 0.5, which is 0.98. 68 minus 0.98 is 67.02. 68 plus 0.98 is 68.98. Put the two intervals next to each other. 67.02 to 68.98 is wider than 67.18 to 68.82. To be more confident of catching the mean, the interval has to reach farther. The likely mistake is thinking a higher confidence level makes a tighter, better interval. It makes a wider one.

Example 8.5 · same exams, 90%

A larger sample makes a smaller error bound

n = 36

Error bound 0.8225

The original sample. The interval is 67.18 to 68.82.

n = 100

Error bound 0.4935

1.645 × 3 / 10 = 0.4935. The interval gets narrower.

n = 25

Error bound 0.987

1.645 × 3 / 5 = 0.987. The interval gets wider.

The sample mean stays 68. The standard deviation stays 3. Only n changes.

DESMOS ↗  1.645*3/sqrt(100)  and 1.645*3/sqrt(25)

Leave the mean, the standard deviation, and the 90% level alone. Change only the sample size. When n is 100, the square root of 100 is 10, so the error bound is 1.645 times 3 divided by 10, which the book gives as 0.4935. When n is 25, the square root of 25 is 5, so the error bound is 1.645 times 3 divided by 5, which the book gives as 0.987. A larger sample makes the standard error smaller, so the interval is narrower. A smaller sample does the opposite. The likely mistake is changing the sample mean when n changes. In this example the book keeps the sample mean at 68. Example 8.7, if the hour remains: sigma is 15 years, the error bound they want is 2, z is 1.96, and n = (1.96 times 15 divided by 2) squared = 216.09. Round up to 217 students.

Your turn · Try It 8.2 · 3 minutes

Pizza delivery, 90%

The population standard deviation is 6 minutes. A sample of 28 restaurants has mean delivery time 36 minutes. Find the 90% interval.

Start at 36. Move the error bound both ways.

1.645 × 6 / √28 = 1.865

34.13 to 37.87 minutes

Try It 8.2. Three minutes. The question is on the slide. The worked line is hidden until the fragment. z is 1.645. The standard error is 6 divided by the square root of 28. The error bound is 1.645 times that, which is 1.865. 36 minus 1.865 is 34.135, which rounds to 34.13. 36 plus 1.865 is 37.865, which rounds to 37.87. The likely mistake is dividing by 28 instead of the square root of 28, or walking only one direction from 36.
