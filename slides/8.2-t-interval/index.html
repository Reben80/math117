MATH 117   Unit 5 · Confidence intervals

When sigma is unknown, use s

The sample standard deviation replaces σ.
The cutoff is a t-score. The degrees of freedom are n − 1.

SECTION 8.2

Prof. Rebin Muhammad

Montgomery College

Say: yesterday sigma was known, so the cutoff was z. Today the population standard deviation is unknown. Use s, the sample standard deviation. The cutoff is a t-score. The degrees of freedom are n minus 1. Point at the interval 7.30 to 9.15. That is Example 8.8, a range for the mean sensory rate. Do not give a lecture on the shape of the t curve. One sentence is enough: the t cutoff sits a little farther out than z, because s is an estimate. About one class period. The likely mistake is still dividing by n, or using z when the problem gives s.

The words, then the symbols

s estimates sigma. t replaces z.

$$\mathrm{EBM} = t\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$$

s is the sample standard deviation. It estimates σ, which is unknown here. The degrees of freedom are df = n − 1. t is the cutoff on that t-distribution. The middle area is the confidence level.

The standard error is s over the square root of n. The interval is still the sample mean minus the error bound, to the sample mean plus the error bound.

Desmos: tdist(df).inversecdf(area to the left). For 95%, the left area is 0.975. For 90%, it is 0.95.

DESMOS ↗  tdist(14).inversecdf(0.975)  the 95% cutoff, df = 14

Name s before the formula. s is the sample standard deviation. It stands in for sigma. The degrees of freedom are n minus 1, because the deviations from the sample mean have to add to zero, so the last one is fixed. Say that once. Do not ask them to memorize a derivation. t has the same job z had. For a 95% interval the two tails share 5%, so each tail is 0.025, and the area to the left of the positive cutoff is 0.975. In Desmos, tdist(14).inversecdf(0.975) is about 2.145. The z cutoff for 95% was 1.96. t is a bit farther out, so the interval is a bit wider than a z interval would have been. The likely mistake is typing inversecdf(0.95) for a 95% interval. 0.95 would leave 5% in the right tail only, which is a 90% interval. The interval still runs both ways from the sample mean.

Example 8.8 · acupuncture, 15 subjects

Mean 8.2267, s = 1.6722

THE 15 SENSORY RATES

8.6, 9.4, 7.9, 6.8, 8.3, 7.3, 9.2, 9.6, 8.7, 11.4, 10.3, 5.4, 8.1, 5.5, 6.9

The book’s interval is (7.30, 9.15). A calculator TInterval prints (7.3006, 9.1527).

We estimate with 95% confidence that the true mean sensory rate is between 7.30 and 9.15.

Fifteen rates, so the degrees of freedom are 14. The book’s sample mean is 8.2267 and the sample standard deviation is 1.6722. The book’s error bound is 0.9240. 8.2267 minus 0.9240 is 7.3027, and the book rounds the interval to (7.30, 9.15). 8.2267 plus 0.9240 is 9.1507. A calculator TInterval prints (7.3006, 9.1527). Use the book’s (7.30, 9.15) in the sentence. Desmos tdist(14).inversecdf(0.975) is about 2.145. Times 1.6722, divided by the square root of 15, is about 0.926, close to the book’s 0.9240. The small gap is rounding. Read the sentence: we estimate with 95% confidence that the true mean sensory rate is between 7.30 and 9.15. The likely mistake is using z = 1.96 because the level is 95%. This problem gives a sample standard deviation, so the cutoff is t.

Example 8.9 · chemicals in cord blood

Mean 127.45, s = 25.965, n = 20

127.45 − 10.038 = 117.412 and 127.45 + 10.038 = 137.488.

The calculator prints (117.41, 137.49). We estimate with 90% confidence that the mean number of chemicals is between 117.412 and 137.488.

DESMOS ↗  tdist(19).inversecdf(0.95)  the book’s t is 1.729

Twenty infants, so df is 19. The book’s mean is 127.45 and s is 25.965. The level is 90%, so each tail is 0.05, and the area to the left is 0.95. The book’s t is 1.729. The error bound is 1.729 times 25.965 divided by the square root of 20. The book gives 10.038. 127.45 minus 10.038 is 117.412. 127.45 plus 10.038 is 137.488. The calculator prints (117.41, 137.49). The sentence: we estimate with 90% confidence that the mean number of targeted chemicals is between 117.412 and 137.488. The likely mistake is inversecdf(0.90), which is the wrong tail, or dividing by 20 instead of the square root of 20.

n = 12, so df = 11. The left area for a 95% cutoff is 0.975.

2.201 × 1.2904 / √12 = 0.820

8.16 to 9.80 hours

Try It 8.8. The book prints the twelve times and does not print the worked answer. The fragment is the same arithmetic as Example 8.8. The mean of the twelve times is 8.9833. The sample standard deviation is 1.2904. df is 11. tdist(11).inversecdf(0.975) is 2.201. The error bound is 2.201 times 1.2904 divided by the square root of 12, which is 0.820. 8.9833 minus 0.820 is 8.16. 8.9833 plus 0.820 is 9.80. The likely mistake is df = 12, or inversecdf(0.95) for a 95% interval.
