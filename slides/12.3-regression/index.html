A point, and the point on the line

The gap is called a residual

y-hat

\(\hat{y}\)

The value on the line. Say it “y-hat.” It is not, usually, the y in the table.

Residual

\(y - \hat{y}\)

The vertical gap. Positive means the point is above the line.

SSE

Add the squared gaps

The line we use is the one that makes this sum as small as it can be.

There are 11 points, so there are 11 residuals. Add the squared gaps:

$$\mathrm{SSE} = \sum (y-\hat{y})^{2}$$

Any other line would have a larger SSE. This line is the least-squares line.

ŷ is read y-hat. Residual ε = y − ŷ, epsilon. Above the line, the residual is positive and the line underestimated y. Below the line, the residual is negative and the line overestimated y. SSE is the sum of the squared residuals. Minimizing SSE is the least-squares criterion.

The formulas

Where a and b come from

$$\hat{y} = a + bx$$

$$b = \dfrac{\sum (x-\bar{x})(y-\bar{y})}{\sum (x-\bar{x})^{2}}$$ $$a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x}$$

$$b = r\,\dfrac{s_y}{s_x}$$

You do not add these sums by hand. Desmos does. The exact line passes through the two means, \(\bar{x} = 69.18\) and \(\bar{y} = 160.45\).

The correlation coefficient

r measures the line

The number

\(-1 \le r \le 1\)

Close to 1 or −1 is a strong line. Close to 0, the best line is flat: x does not predict y. A curve can still be there, so check the plot.

The sign

Same sign as b

Positive r: x and y climb together. Negative r: one climbs while the other falls.

These exams

\(r = 0.6631\)

Positive, and moderate. The points climb, with a visible scatter.

Strong correlation does not mean x causes y. Correlation does not imply causation. Desmos reports \(r^{2}\). The square root has the same sign as \(b\).

Book: r is always between −1 and 1. r = 1 is perfect positive, r = −1 is perfect negative, and in both cases every point is on the line. r = 0 means no linear correlation, but a curve can also give r near 0, so look at the plot. The sign of r matches the sign of b. For the exams the book gives r = 0.6631. The note in the book: strong correlation does not suggest that x causes y.

The coefficient of determination

\(r^{2}\) is the share the line explains

$$r^{2} = 0.6631^{2} = 0.4397 \approx 44\%$$

About 44% of the variation in the final exams can be explained by the variation in the third exams, using this line.

The other 56% is the scatter the line misses. \(1 - 0.44 = 0.56\). 44% is not an exam score, and it is not the percent of points that sit on the line.

DESMOS ↗  Desmos shows R², not r   ·   r = √R², negative when b is negative

Book: r² = 0.6631² = 0.4397. Approximately 44% of the variation in the final-exam grades is explained by the variation in the third-exam grades. Approximately 56% is not. 1 − 0.44 = 0.56. Desmos shows R² on the regression, not r. Take the square root and give it the sign of b.
