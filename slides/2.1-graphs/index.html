Section 2.1

Three pictures

01

Stem-and-leaf

Every value stays on the page. Use it when the list is short.

02

Line graph

A point for each value, connected. The height is how often it happened.

03

Bar graph

Separate bars, one per category. The bars do not touch.

A graph shows where the data cluster, and where they thin out.

Descriptive statistics is this chapter: display the data, then describe the center and the spread. Graph first, then calculate.

Example 2.2

One distance sits alone

Kilometers from home to a supermarket. The leaf is the digit after the decimal.

Data: 1.1, 1.5, 2.3, 2.5, 2.7, 3.2, 3.3, 3.3, 3.5, 3.8, 4.0, 4.2, 4.5, 4.5, 4.7, 4.8, 5.5, 5.6, 6.5, 6.7, 12.3. Twenty-one values. The book prints empty stems from 7 through 11 so the gap shows. An outlier does not fit the pattern. It may be a recording mistake, such as 50 written for 500, or it may be real. Outliers return later in the chapter. Try It 2.2, miles to campus, has 8.0 as the value to discuss.

Example 2.4 · a line graph

How often each answer came up

Forty parents. How many times a week must you remind a teenager to do chores?

The points are connected. The peak is 3 reminders, said by 14 of the 40 parents. The frequencies add to 40.

Table 2.7. Frequencies 2, 5, 8, 14, 7, 4 for 0 through 5 reminders. Try It 2.4 is car repairs in a year: frequencies 7, 10, 14, 9 for 0 through 3 visits. Same kind of graph.

Example 2.5 · a bar graph

The bars stay apart

Percent of U.S. TikTok users in each age group.

Ages 10–19 are the largest share, 32.5%. A gap between bars means these are categories, not one measured scale.

Table 2.9: 10–19 is 32.5%, 20–29 is 29.5%, 30–39 is 16.4%, 40–49 is 13.9%, 50+ is 7.1%. The percents add to 99.4 because of rounding. Try It 2.5, Park City: children 19%, working-age adults 43%, retirees 38%. Example 2.6, high school graduates in 2030: Northeast 16.1%, Midwest 21.6%, South 39.0%, West 23.3%. A histogram, section 2.2, is the graph whose bars touch.
