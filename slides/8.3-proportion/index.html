A share of the population

The sample proportion is successes divided by n.
The margin of error reaches both ways from that share.

Say: a proportion is a count of successes divided by the sample size. 421 of 500 is 0.842, the sample proportion. That is the point estimate for the population proportion. The interval 0.810 to 0.874 is a share of all adults in the city, not a count of people in the sample. The symbol in the book is p-prime, also read p-hat. Call it the sample proportion. Use the interval when the successes and the failures are both more than 5. 421 and 79 both clear that. About one class period. Plus four is one example, not a new course.

The words, then the symbols

Sample proportion, then the error bound

$$\hat p=\frac{x}{n}\qquad \mathrm{EBP}=z\sqrt{\frac{\hat p\hat q}{n}}$$

x is the number of successes. n is the sample size. p-hat is the sample proportion, the point estimate for p. q-hat is 1 minus p-hat, the share of failures.

EBP is the error bound for the proportion. The interval is p-hat minus EBP to p-hat plus EBP.

Use this when the successes and the failures are both more than 5. z is the same cutoff as in section 8.1. For 95% the book uses 1.96. For 90% it uses 1.645.

Say p-hat before the formula. p-hat is x divided by n, the sample proportion. It estimates p, the population proportion. q-hat is 1 minus p-hat. The book also writes p-prime. Either name is the sample proportion. EBP is the error bound for the proportion. It uses p-hat and q-hat because the true p is unknown. To build the interval, start at p-hat and move EBP both ways. The middle of a finished interval is p-hat. The check before you start: successes greater than 5, and failures greater than 5. The likely mistake is putting the count 421 into the interval, or using sigma from the mean formula.

Example 8.10 · adults in a large city

421 of 500 own a smartphone

1.96 × the square root of (0.842 × 0.158 / 500) = 0.032.

0.842 − 0.032 = 0.810 and 0.842 + 0.032 = 0.874.

The calculator prints (0.81003, 0.87397). Between 81% and 87.4%.

DESMOS ↗  421/500  then the error bound with z = 1.96

421 successes out of 500. The sample proportion is 421 divided by 500, which is 0.842. The failures are 79, and 0.158 is 1 minus 0.842. Both 421 and 79 are more than 5. The level is 95%, so the book uses z = 1.96. The error bound is 1.96 times the square root of 0.842 times 0.158 divided by 500, which is 0.03197, and the slide rounds that factor to 0.032. 0.842 minus 0.032 is 0.810. 0.842 plus 0.032 is 0.874. The calculator, without that rounding, prints (0.81003, 0.87397). The book’s sentence: we estimate with 95% confidence that between 81% and 87.4% of all adult residents of this city have smartphones. The likely mistake is reporting 421, or 84%, as the interval.

300 of 500 students are registered voters

0.60 − 0.036 = 0.564 and 0.60 + 0.036 = 0.636. The book’s interval is (0.564, 0.636).

We estimate with 90% confidence that between 56.4% and 63.6% of all students are registered voters.

300 of 500 is 0.60. The failures are 200. Both are more than 5. The level is 90%, so z is 1.645. The error bound is 1.645 times the square root of 0.60 times 0.40 divided by 500, which is 0.036. 0.60 minus 0.036 is 0.564. 0.60 plus 0.036 is 0.636. The book’s interval is (0.564, 0.636), and the calculator agrees. Two wordings from the book: between 56.4% and 63.6% of all students are registered voters. The likely mistake is a 95% cutoff, 1.96, on a 90% problem.

Example 8.12 · the plus-four method

Add two successes and two failures

The sample

6 of 25 smoked

x = 6 and n = 25. The book uses plus four because the sample is small.

Plus four

x = 8, n = 29

Add two successes and two failures. 6 + 2 = 8 and 25 + 4 = 29.

95% interval

0.113 to 0.439

8/29 = 0.276. The book’s error bound is 0.163.

0.276 − 0.163 = 0.113 and 0.276 + 0.163 = 0.439.

We are 95% confident that the true proportion who smoke is between 0.113 and 0.439.

Name the method: plus four. Six of 25 students smoked. The book adds two successes and two failures, so x becomes 8 and n becomes 29. 8 divided by 29 is 0.2759, and the book uses 0.276. The book’s error bound is 0.163. 0.276 minus 0.163 is 0.113. 0.276 plus 0.163 is 0.439. The calculator interval is (0.113, 0.439). The sentence: we are 95% confident that the true proportion of all statistics students who smoke is between 0.113 and 0.439. The likely mistake is using 6 and 25 after announcing plus four, or adding four successes.
