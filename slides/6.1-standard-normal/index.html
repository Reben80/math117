Before any formula

The center, the step, the pattern

The mean

The center

The same mean as Unit 2. On the bell it sits under the highest point.

The standard deviation

One step

A typical distance from the center. A bigger step makes a wider bell.

The distribution

The pattern

Normal means the bell. Most values are near the mean. Few are far out.

Say the three words out loud and tie each one to Unit 2. Mean is the center. Standard deviation is one step. Distribution means the pattern, not a formula. Ask: where do most of the values sit? Near the middle. If someone asks what X means later, say X is one value drawn from this pattern. Do not build a definition of a random variable.

The only new arithmetic

How many steps, and which value

HOW MANY STEPS

$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$

μ is the mean. σ is one step. Positive is above the mean. Negative is below it.

WHICH VALUE

$$x = \mu + z\sigma$$

Start at the mean. Add the steps. One step above the mean is z = 1.

Read \(X \sim N(\mu, \sigma)\) as: a bell, center μ, step σ. The book’s check: center 5 and step 2 puts 11 at z = 3, because 5 + 3(2) = 11.

Say: z is not a new kind of data. It is the number of steps from the mean. Do the book’s check on the board: start at 5, three steps of 2, land on 11. Then read the shorthand once: X ~ N(μ, σ) means a normal bell with that center and that step. Z ~ N(0, 1) is the bell whose center is 0 and whose step is 1. Watch for: students treating μ and σ as two more variables to solve for. They are given. x is the value. z is the steps.

Example 6.1

Mean 5, one step is 6

The value 17

z = 2

Two steps of 6 above 5. Check it: 5 + 2(6) = 17.

The value 1

z = −0.67

Below 5. (1 − 5) / 6 is −0.666…, and the book rounds to −0.67.

The sign

Which side

Positive means above the mean. Negative means below the mean.

DESMOS  (17-5)/6    (1-5)/6

Say: cover the formula and ask how many steps of 6 it takes to get from 5 to 17. Two. Then show (17−5)/6 = 2 so the formula matches the count. For 1, the subtraction comes out negative, so the value is below the mean. (1−5)/6 = −0.666…, book −0.67. The check 5 + (−0.67)(6) is about 1, not exactly 1, because −0.67 was rounded. Watch for: a positive 0.67. The sign is the side.

Example 6.2 · pounds lost in a month

Average loss 5, one step is 2

LOST 10 POUNDS

z = 2.5

2.5 standard deviations to the right of the mean 5.

GAINED 3 POUNDS

z = −4

A gain is x = −3. Four standard deviations to the left of 5.

DESMOS  (10-5)/2    (-3-5)/2

Say: the average person loses 5 pounds. One step is 2 pounds. Ten pounds lost is 2.5 steps above that average, (10−5)/2 = 2.5. A gain of 3 pounds is a negative loss, so the value is −3, not 3. (−3−5)/2 = −4, four steps below the average. Watch for: students who use +3 and get (3−5)/2 = −1. Ask which side of 'no change' a gain sits on. The book calls the variable weight lost, so a gain is negative.

The same z can be a different x

First group

Second group

4 pounds, z = 2

Jerome

Your turn · Try It 6.1 · 3 minutes

One value, one z-score

The center is 12 and one step is 3. How many steps is the value 1 from 12, and which side?

$$z = \frac{1 - 12}{3}$$

Find z. Then say whether 1 is left or right of 12.

DESMOS  (1-12)/3

Try It 6.1. The book writes X ~ N(12, 3). Walk it: 1 − 12 = −11, then divide by the step 3. z = −11/3 = −3.67, to the left of 12. A student who says 3.67 forgot the sign. A student who says −11 forgot to divide by 3. Give them three minutes, then show the next slide.

Try It 6.1

z = −3.67

$$\frac{1-12}{3} = -\frac{11}{3} = -3.67$$

The value 1 is 3.67 steps to the left of the center 12. The sign is the side.

Say the arithmetic once, slowly: 1 minus 12 is negative 11. Negative 11 divided by 3 is negative 3.67. Left of 12. Then sit down and go to the heights.

Example 6.3 · young men in Chile, 2009–2010

Mean height 170 cm, step 6.28

168 CM

z = −0.32

A little below average. 0.32 of a step to the left of 170.

z = 1.27

177.98 cm

1.27 steps above 170. The book’s height is 177.98 cm.

DESMOS  (168-170)/6.28    170+1.27*6.28

Say: same two moves, and now the step is not a whole number. From a height, count the steps. From the steps, recover the height. (168−170)/6.28 = −0.318…, and the book rounds to −0.32. 170 + 1.27(6.28) = 177.98 cm. If someone asks about Example 6.4: a 1984 height and a 2009 height can both be z = −1.5, so they are equally far below their own year’s average. Example 6.5 · the 68-95-99.7 rule

Mean 50, standard deviation 6

About 68%

44 to 56

About 68 out of 100 values, within one step of 50.

About 95%

38 to 62

Within two steps. The z-scores are −2 and +2.

About 99.7%

32 to 68

Within three steps. Almost every value. Almost nobody is farther out.

50 − 6 = 44 and 50 + 6 = 56. The other two rows use 12 and 18.

Say: this percent is a share of the people, not a test score. About 68 out of 100 values sit between 44 and 56. Hold your hands near the middle for 68, wider for 95, and almost the whole curve for 99.7. 50 − 6 = 44 and 50 + 6 = 56. Two steps is 12, so 38 to 62. Three steps is 18, so 32 to 68. Example 6.6 is the same rule on the 1984 Chile heights, mean 172.36 and step 6.34: 166.02 to 178.7, then 159.68 to 185.04, then 153.34 to 191.38. Leave it unless you have time.
