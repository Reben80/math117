Levels of measurement

From names up to ratios

1
Nominal
Names. No order. Favorite food, or a yes/no answer. You cannot calculate with them.
2
Ordinal
An order, and no measured gap. Excellent, good, satisfactory, unsatisfactory.
3
Interval
Differences make sense. Zero is not “none.” 100°F is 60° more than 40°F. 0°F is not the absence of heat.
4
Ratio
A true zero, so ratios make sense. Scores 80 and 20. Zero means no points. 80 is four times 20.
Nominal and ordinal cannot be used in calculations. Interval has meaningful differences. Ratio has a real zero. Exam scores in the book are 80, 68, 20, and 92. 80 is four times 20.

0.55 means 55% work 4 hours or fewer. The last cumulative entry is 1.

Rounding rule, if it comes up: the final answer goes one decimal past the data. (4+6+9)/3 = 6.3. Do not round the steps in the middle. Relative frequencies may miss 1 by a rounding hair. They should be close.

Table 1.13 · grouped

Heights of 100 soccer players

Each bar is a two-inch interval. The tall bar is 66 to 68 inches, 40 players.

Shorter than 65.95 inches is the first three bars: 5 + 3 + 15 = 23%.

Example 1.14. 23% is the cumulative relative frequency in the third row. Example 1.15: between 61.95 and 65.95 inches is 0.03 + 0.15 = 18%. Heights are quantitative continuous.
