Example 2.23 · 40 student heights

Which quarter is the narrow one?

The four quarters span 5.5, 1.5, 4, and 7 inches. The second quarter is the narrow one. The fourth is the widest. Range = maximum − minimum = 77 − 59 = 18. IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 70 − 64.5 = 5.5.

Minimum 59, Q1 64.5, median 66, Q3 70, maximum 77. Each quarter holds about 25% of the heights. The book also notes that 59 through 65 holds more than 25%, so it holds more heights than 66 through 70. In Desmos: L = [the 40 heights], then min, quartile(L, 1), median, quartile(L, 3), max, or boxplot(L).

Fifteen students · minutes of exercise

300 minutes is past the fence

Min 0 · Q1 20 · median 40 · Q3 60 · max 300

IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 60 − 20 = 40.

Upper fence = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR = 60 + 60 = 120.

300 is greater than 120, so it is a potential outlier.

About 75% of these students exercise 60 minutes or less. Half of them fall between 20 and 60 minutes. Fifteen students is a small sample. The principal should ask more students before buying equipment on this alone.

The fifteen times: 0, 40, 60, 30, 60, 10, 45, 30, 300, 90, 30, 120, 60, 0, 20. If 300 is set aside, the book’s new maximum is 120. Q1 is still 20 and Q3 is still 60. The conclusion does not change: half the students are between 20 and 60 minutes. The next slide is the discussion, then Try It 2.22.
