Section 1.1

Tell a parameter
from a statistic

01

Name the groups

The population is everyone the question is about. The sample is the part you actually measure.

02

Name the numbers

The parameter describes the population. The statistic describes the sample, and it estimates the parameter.

You will also name the variable, the data, and whether the number is a mean or a proportion.

This is textbook section 1.1. Blackboard tags this section with learning outcome 1.2, compare a parameter with a statistic. Textbook section 1.2 has not started.

The word

Statistics has four jobs

01

Collect

Gather the measurements.

02

Analyze

Organize what you gathered.

03

Interpret

Decide what the data is saying.

04

Present

Show the result clearly.

A calculator can do the arithmetic. Understanding the data is the point.

Do not linger. The assessed skill is the six labels, not this definition.

Two jobs

Summarize, or conclude

DESCRIPTIVE

Summarize what you have

A graph, or a number such as an average. You are describing the data in front of you.

INFERENTIAL

Say something about everyone

Use a sample to estimate a population. Probability tells you how confident that estimate can be.

PROBABILITY, IN ONE MINUTE

A fair coin has probability 1/2. A few tosses wander. Karl Pearson tossed a coin 24,000 times and got 12,012 heads.

Probability is a preview. The course returns to it later. Pearson: 24,000 tosses, 12,012 heads, about half.

Every study

Six words

01

Population

The whole group the question is about.

02

Parameter

A number that describes that whole group.

03

Sample

The part of the group you actually measure.

04

Statistic

A number from the sample. It estimates the parameter.

05

Variable

The characteristic you record for one member. Written X.

06

Data

The values you get. One value is a datum.

Same six labels, every problem, in this order.

Section 1.1

The sample has to look
like the population

01

A school’s GPA

You estimate it from some students. You do not open every transcript.

02

A national poll

1,000 to 2,000 people stand in for the whole country.

03

A few cans

A few 16-ounce cans stand in for the whole production run.

If the sample misses part of the population, the statistic is a weak estimate.

Still section 1.1. How a sample is chosen is textbook section 1.2. This slide only says the sample must represent the population.

Same study

The variable is not the data

VARIABLE

X = the amount

The amount one first-year student spent on supplies. A numerical variable has units, so you can average it. A categorical variable is a label, such as major or yes/no. Averaging the labels does not make sense.

DATA

$150, $200, $225

These are three of the values. X is the variable. 150 is one datum. The mean of all 100 values is the statistic.

Two kinds of number

A mean, or a proportion

MEAN

For a numerical variable

86,  75,  92

(86 + 75 + 92) / 3 = 84.3

In everyday speech, “average” means this mean.

PROPORTION

For a yes / no split

22 men out of 40

22 / 40 = 0.55

Either kind of number can be a parameter or a statistic. The difference is who it describes.

Example · a proportion

Malpractice suits

An insurer selects 500 doctors at random from a professional directory.

POPULATION

All doctors listed in that directory

PARAMETER

The proportion who have been in a malpractice suit

SAMPLE

The 500 doctors selected

STATISTIC

That proportion inside the 500

VARIABLE

X = whether one doctor has been in a suit

DATA

Yes, or no

Watch these

Where the labels get swapped

01

The sample is not the population

“Students in this class” is not “all math students.”

02

Parameter, then statistic

The parameter is everyone. The statistic is the people you measured. A sample mean of 3.5 days is a statistic.

03

X is not a data value

$150 is one datum, not the statistic. On a proportion, the data are yes and no.
