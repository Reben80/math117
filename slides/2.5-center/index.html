The mean

Add the values, then divide

SAMPLE MEAN

x̄

The people you actually measured.

POPULATION MEAN

μ

Everyone the study is about.

Mean = (sum of the values) / n.

μ uses the same formula for the whole population. When someone says average, they mean this mean.

DESMOS  L = [your values]  →  mean(L)

Let Desmos do the adding: L = [values], then mean(L). That is x-bar. The Law of Large Numbers is the next idea: larger samples, and the sample mean is very likely to move toward μ. The book returns to that later. One sentence is enough today.

Example 2.26 · 40 patients

The median has a location

40 AGES, ALREADY IN ORDER

1–10: 3, 4, 8, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15
11–20: 15, 16, 16, 17, 17, 18, 21, 22, 22, 24
21–30: 24, 25, 26, 26, 27, 27, 29, 29, 31, 32
31–40: 33, 33, 34, 34, 35, 37, 40, 44, 44, 47

Location of the median = (n + 1) / 2

(40 + 1) / 2 = 20.5. The median is (24 + 24) / 2 = 24. The mean is 23.6.

The 20th and 21st ages are the two 24s, at the end of row 11–20 and the start of row 21–30. The location 20.5 is not an age.

DESMOS  L = [the 40 ages]  →  median(L) gives 24, mean(L) gives 23.6

Ordered ages: 3, 4, 8, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 15, 16, 16, 17, 17, 18, 21, 22, 22, 24, 24, 25, 26, 26, 27, 27, 29, 29, 31, 32, 33, 33, 34, 34, 35, 37, 40, 44, 44, 47. Position 20 is 24. Position 21 is 24. Desmos: mean(L) = 23.6, median(L) = 24. If n is odd, the location (n+1)/2 is a single ordered value. If n is even, average the two middle values.

Spot the mistake · 3 minutes

“The median age is 20.5.”

For the 40 patients, (40 + 1) / 2 = 20.5.

40 AGES, ALREADY IN ORDER

1–10: 3, 4, 8, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15  ·  11–20: 15, 16, 16, 17, 17, 18, 21, 22, 22, 24
21–30: 24, 25, 26, 26, 27, 27, 29, 29, 31, 32  ·  31–40: 33, 33, 34, 34, 35, 37, 40, 44, 44, 47

1   What is 20.5?

✓ A location. It sits between the 20th and 21st ages.

2   What is the median age?

✓ 24. The last age in row 11–20 and the first in row 21–30 are both 24.

3   What about the mean?

✓ 23.6 years. Close to 24, not to 20.5.

Right-arrow once per answer. The location is not the median. Read the age that sits at that location.

One income pulls the mean

49 people earn this.

$30,000

The 20 scores: 50, 53, 59, 59, 63, 63, 72, 72, 72, 72, 72, 76, 78, 81, 83, 84, 84, 84, 90, 93. 84 appears three times, so 72 is the only mode. Try It 2.28, books checked out, the mode is 7, which appears four times. Try It 2.29, credit scores 680, 680, 700, 720, 720, is also bimodal.

Think it through

The ad says the average loss is 6 pounds

The book’s example: the mode can be 2 pounds.

1   Which center did the ad pick?

✓ The mean.

2   What can the mode be saying?

✓ The most common loss can be much smaller than 6.

3   What would you ask for next?

✓ The median, and how many people are in the average.

The 6 pounds and the mode of 2 pounds are the book’s weight-loss illustration, already on the previous slide. Do not invent a new data set.

One more sentence

Larger samples pull the mean toward μ

The Law of Large Numbers: take larger and larger samples, and the sample mean is very likely to get closer to the population mean.

The book returns to this later. Today, the point is what the mean is.

In practice Desmos does the arithmetic. Read the mean and the median, then say what they tell you.

Before section 2.6

You can name the center

The mean is the arithmetic average. Outliers pull it.

The median is the middle of the ordered list. Its location is (n + 1) / 2.

The mode is the most common value, and it works for words.
