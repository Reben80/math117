Linear equations

A straight line, written
\(y = a + bx\).

SECTION 12.1

Prof. Rebin Muhammad

Montgomery College

About 20 minutes. This section is the algebra the rest of the unit sits on. No data yet. y = a + bx is the book's form: a is the intercept, b is the slope. Say the intercept as 'a' and the slope as 'b'.

The form of a line

\(y = a + bx\)

x · independent

You choose it.

Hours, age, or a third-exam score. You substitute a value for x.

y · dependent

The equation gives it.

Cost, vocabulary, or a final-exam score. Put in an x. The equation gives y.

a and b

Start, then tilt.

a is the y-intercept, the value of y when x = 0. b is the slope.

One step to the right changes y by b. Any non-vertical line can be written this way.

Book: the variable x is the independent variable, and y is the dependent variable. Typically you choose a value for x and then solve for y. b = slope and a = y-intercept. The intercept is the y-coordinate of the point (0, a).

Example 12.3 · a tax return

A fee, plus an hourly rate

THE EQUATION

$$y = 31.50 + 32x$$

x is the hours the job takes. y is the dollars the customer pays.

31.50 is the fee for showing up. 32 is one more hour. Two hours: \(31.50 + 32(2) = 95.50\).

DESMOS ↗  y = 31.50 + 32x

Example 12.3. $32 per hour plus a $31.50 one-time charge. y = 31.50 + 32x. Fixed cost 31.50, and (32)(x) is the processing.
