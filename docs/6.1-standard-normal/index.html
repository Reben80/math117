Before any formula

The center, the step, the pattern

The mean is the center of the distribution. The standard deviation tells how spread out the values are. One standard deviation is one step away from the mean.

Point at the peak: that is the mean, the center. The width of the bell is the standard deviation, how spread out the values are. Then say one standard deviation is one step from the mean, left or right. The shaded middle is where most values sit. The tails are the few. Ask: where do most of the values sit? Near the mean. Watch for: calling the spread a step before connecting it to the standard deviation from Unit 2. If someone asks what X means later, say x is an individual value from this pattern. Do not build a definition of a random variable.

The only new arithmetic

How many standard deviations, and which value

HOW MANY STANDARD DEVIATIONS

$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$

μ population mean. σ population standard deviation. x an individual value. z how many standard deviations from the mean. Positive is above the mean. Negative is below.

WHICH VALUE

$$x = \mu + z\sigma$$

Start at the mean. Move z standard deviations.

Read \(X \sim N(\mu, \sigma)\) as a bell with that mean and that standard deviation.

Say: z is not a new measurement. It is how many standard deviations the individual value x is from the mean. μ is the population mean. σ is the population standard deviation. They are given. Positive z is above the mean. Negative z is below the mean. To find z, subtract the mean and divide by σ. To find x, start at the mean and add z times σ. The number line is mean 5, standard deviation 2. x = 5 is z = 0, x = 7 is z = 1, x = 9 is z = 2, x = 11 is z = 3. Each move of 2 is one standard deviation. On the board: (11 − 5) / 2 = 3, and 5 + 3(2) = 11. Watch for: forgetting to divide by σ, so 11 − 5 = 6 and they call that z. Read X ~ N(μ, σ) once, as a bell with that mean and that standard deviation. Do not ask them to memorize the tilde.

Example 6.1

Mean 5, standard deviation 6

17 is z = 2. The standard deviation is 6, so 17 is two steps above the mean 5. Check: 5 + 2(6) = 17.

1 is z = −0.67. (1 − 5) / 6 = −0.666…, below the mean. The book rounds to −0.67.

A positive z-score is above the mean. A negative z-score is below the mean.

DESMOS  (17-5)/6    (1-5)/6

The peak is the mean, 5. The standard deviation is 6. Point at 17, two standard deviations above the mean: (17−5)/6 = 2. The check is 5 + 2(6) = 17. Point at 1, on the left of the mean: (1−5)/6 = −0.666…, and the book rounds to −0.67. A positive z-score is above the mean. A negative z-score is below the mean. Watch for: a positive 0.67, which drops the sign, or z = 12, which forgets to divide by σ. The check 5 + (−0.67)(6) is about 1, not exactly 1, because −0.67 was rounded.

Example 6.2 · pounds lost in a month

Mean loss 5, standard deviation 2

DESMOS  (10-5)/2    (-3-5)/2

The mean is an average loss of 5 pounds. The standard deviation is 2 pounds. Ten pounds lost is above the mean: (10−5)/2 = 2.5. A positive z-score is above the mean. A gain of 3 pounds is a negative loss, so the value is −3, not +3. (−3−5)/2 = −4, below the mean. A negative z-score is below the mean. Watch for: treating a gain of 3 pounds as +3 and getting (3−5)/2 = −1. Ask which side of no change a gain sits on. The book calls the variable weight lost, so a gain is negative.

Example 6.2 · two different scales

The same z can be a different x

17 pounds and 4 pounds are different values. Both are 2 standard deviations above their own mean, so both have z = 2. The same z-score is the same standardized distance, even when the original values differ. Jerome’s 10 is 1.5 standard deviations below the mean 16, so z = −1.5.

Three bells, three means. 17 pounds: mean 5, standard deviation 6, (17−5)/6 = 2. 4 pounds: mean 2, standard deviation 1, (4−2)/1 = 2. The original values differ. The z-score is the same, so each value is the same standardized distance: two standard deviations above its own mean. Jerome’s mean is 16 and his score is 10. (10−16)/4 = −1.5, below the mean. The book already gives z = −1.5. Watch for: deciding 17 and 4 cannot both be z = 2 because the numbers differ, or flipping Jerome’s sign to +1.5. The book’s shorthand is X ~ N(5, 6) and Y ~ N(2, 1). Read it if you write it down. Do not ask them to memorize the tilde.

Example 6.3 · young men in Chile, 2009–2010

Mean height 170 cm, standard deviation 6.28

(168 − 170) / 6.28 = −0.32, below the mean.    170 + 1.27(6.28) = 177.98 cm, above the mean.

DESMOS  (168-170)/6.28    170+1.27*6.28

The peak is the mean height, 170 cm. The standard deviation is 6.28 cm. 168 is just left of the mean: (168−170)/6.28 = −0.318…, and the book rounds to −0.32. A negative z-score is below the mean. The other direction starts from z and finds the value: 170 + 1.27(6.28) = 177.98 cm, above the mean. Watch for: subtracting in the wrong order and reporting +0.32, or adding 1.27 to 170 without multiplying by σ. If someone asks about Example 6.4: a 1984 height and a 2009 height can both be z = −1.5, the same standardized distance below their own year’s mean. Example 6.5 · the 68-95-99.7 rule

Mean 50, standard deviation 6

For an approximately normal distribution, about 68% of the values fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean, about 95% within 2, and about 99.7% within 3.

Mean 50, standard deviation 6: 68% is 44 to 56, 95% is 38 to 62, and 99.7% is 32 to 68.

The shaded middle is a share of the values, not a test score.

This rule is for an approximately normal distribution. The shaded middle is a share of the values, not a test score. About 68% fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean, about 95% within 2, about 99.7% within 3. Mean 50, standard deviation 6. 50 − 6 = 44 and 50 + 6 = 56, so 68% is 44 to 56. Two standard deviations is 12, so 95% is 38 to 62. Three is 18, so 99.7% is 32 to 68. Hold your hands near the middle, then wider, then almost the whole curve. Watch for: walking only one direction, so 50 to 56 instead of 44 to 56, or reading 68 as a score. Example 6.6 is the same rule on the 1984 Chile heights, mean 172.36 and standard deviation 6.34: 166.02 to 178.7, then 159.68 to 185.04, then 153.34 to 191.38. Leave it unless you have time.
