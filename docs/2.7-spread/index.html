Standard deviation is never negative. It is small when the values hug the mean. In practice, use a calculator. Concentrate on what the number tells you. Select Sx for a sample and σx for a population.

Two supermarkets

Same mean, different spread

STORE A

Mean wait 5 minutes

Standard deviation 2 minutes

The waits sit closer to 5.

STORE B

Mean wait 5 minutes

Standard deviation 4 minutes

The waits are more spread out.

A larger standard deviation means more variation about the mean.

The book uses minutes at the checkout. Do not invent a list of waits. The given facts are the two means and the two standard deviations.

Rosa and Binh, both at store A

How many standard deviations away?

ROSA WAITS 7 MINUTES

5 + (1)(2) = 7

One standard deviation above the mean.

BINH WAITS 1 MINUTE

5 + (−2)(2) = 1

Two standard deviations below the mean.

value = mean + (number of standard deviations) × (standard deviation). Two standard deviations from the mean is a rough flag that a value is far. The shape of the data changes that call.

The number of standard deviations need not be a whole number. For a sample the symbols are x-bar and s. For a population they are μ and σ. The empirical rule, about 95% within two standard deviations for a bell shape, comes later. Do not teach it as a fact for every shape.

Sample or population

Divide by n − 1, or by N

SAMPLE

The variance divides by n − 1. The standard deviation is s. On a calculator, read Sx.

POPULATION

The variance divides by N. The standard deviation is σ. On a calculator, read σx.

The deviations from the mean add to zero, so the calculation squares them. Dividing a sample by n − 1 estimates the population variance better than dividing by n. Let the calculator do that arithmetic.

Variance is in squared units. The standard deviation is the square root, back in the units of the data. s estimates σ when the sample looks like the population. The next slide is the keystroke list. Walk it once with a short list before Example 2.32.

Example 2.32 · a sample of 20 fifth-graders

Mean 10.53 years, s = 0.72

One standard deviation above

10.53 + 0.72 = 11.25

Two standard deviations below

10.53 − 1.44 = 9.09

The ages are a sample, so the variance divides by 19, not by 20. The standard deviation is zero only when every age is the same. A small standard deviation means the ages hug 10.53.

Graph first. For a skewed picture, the five-number summary is more useful than the standard deviation, because the two tails do not have the same spread.

Ages: 9 once, 9.5 twice, 10 four times, 10.5 four times, 11 six times, 11.5 three times. n = 20. The deviation table uses the mean 10.525. The book then rounds the average to 10.53. Sum of f times squared deviations is 9.7375. Sample variance is 9.7375 / 19. s = 0.72 to two decimals. The calculator’s Sx is about 0.715891.
