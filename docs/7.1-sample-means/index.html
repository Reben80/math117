The only new step

Divide by the square root of n

THE STEP FOR AN AVERAGE

$$\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$$

σ is one person’s step. n is how many you average. The book calls this the standard error.

HOW MANY STEPS

$$z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$$

Same move as section 6.1. The step on the bottom is smaller.

THE BOOK’S CHECK

15 / √25 = 3

Mean 90, one person’s step 15, sample of 25. The average has step 3, not 15.

90 + 2(3) = 96

Two steps above the average is 96. The center is still 90.

Say the fraction out loud: sigma over the square root of n. n is how many values go into the average, not how many times the class repeats the experiment. Do the book’s check on the board: 15 divided by the square root of 25 is 15 divided by 5, which is 3. Then two of those steps: 90 + 2(3) = 96. Read the shorthand once: X-bar ~ N(μ, σ/√n) means a bell with the population’s center and this smaller step. Watch for: dividing by 25 instead of by 5. That gives 0.6, and the bell is then much too narrow. Also watch for students who move the center to 90/25.

Example 7.1 · an unknown distribution

Mean 90, step 15, sample of 25

0.6997 is the book’s answer. About 70 out of 100 samples land between 85 and 92.

The step inside Desmos is 15/√25, which is 3. One person would use 15.

DESMOS  normaldist(90, 15/sqrt(25))

Say: the population can be any shape. The averages still make a bell, centered at 90, with step 3. Type normaldist(90, 15/sqrt(25)). Desmos opens the area box. Choose Inner, and set 85 and 92. Desmos shows more decimals. The book rounds to 0.6997, about 70%. Watch for: normaldist(90, 15). That is one person, the chapter 6 bell. The sample size has to get into the step. Part b of the example is the 96 from the previous slide. Two standard errors above 90.

DESMOS  normaldist(2, 0.5/sqrt(50))

Say: a single match averages 2 hours and wanders by half an hour. Between 1.8 and 2.3 hours is ordinary for one match, about 38%. That 38% is the chapter 6 calculation with step 0.5. Example 7.3 · ages of iPad users

Mean 34, step 15, sample of 100

The average’s step is 15/√100 = 1.5. The 95th percentile is 36.5, not a person’s age. The chance the average is over 30 is 0.9962.

DESMOS  normaldist(34, 15/sqrt(100)).inversecdf(0.95)

This slide can wait if 6.2 ran long. Say: 100 users, so the step shrinks from 15 years to 1.5 years. The 95th percentile of the average is 36.5. Ninety-five out of 100 samples have an average age of 36.5 or younger. The trap is using the person’s step. 34 + 1.64(15) is about 59, a person’s age. The average only gets 1.64 steps of 1.5, which is 36.5. The book rounds invNorm to 36.5. Over 30: 30 is (34 − 30) / 1.5 ≈ 2.7 steps below the mean, so almost every sample of 100 averages more than 30. The book’s area is 0.9962.
