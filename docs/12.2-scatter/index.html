About 20 minutes. Example 12.5 is four children. Try It 12.5 is Amelia’s six games. No regression line yet. That is section 12.3. The question here is direction and whether a line even makes sense.

Two variables at once

Each pair becomes a point

The pair

(x, y)

x goes across. y goes up. One student, one game, one child: one point.

Direction

Climb, fall, or neither

High with high is a positive direction. High with low is a negative direction.

A line, later

Only if x explains y

Closeness to a line is the strength. A horizontal line is no relationship.

This is bivariate data. “Bi” means two. The chapter uses one independent variable.

Book: bivariate data, 'bi' for two variables. A clear direction is high with high, or high with low. Strength is how close the points are to a line. Exception: points on a horizontal line are a perfect fit and still show no relationship.

What to notice

Direction, then strength

Positive

High with high

As x grows, y tends to grow. Age and vocabulary climb together.

Negative

High with low

As x grows, y tends to fall. The dive table in 12.3 will do this.

No relationship

A cloud, or a flat line

No direction means no relationship. A horizontal line is a perfect fit, and still no relationship.

Strength is how close the points sit to a line. Close means strong. Spread out means weak. Look at the whole pattern, and at any point that sits off it.

Before the line of best fit

A line is for predicting

Draw a line only when one variable helps explain or predict the other.

If x is independent and y is dependent, the line predicts y for a given x.

Age can sit on the horizontal axis for vocabulary. Hours can sit there for Amelia’s points. The calculation is next, in 12.3.

Book, end of 12.2: we only calculate a regression line if one of the variables helps to explain or predict the other. If x is independent and y is dependent, the line predicts y for a given x.
