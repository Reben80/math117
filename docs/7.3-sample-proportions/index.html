Sampling distribution of p-hat

Center at p. Spread shrinks with n.

MEAN

μp^​​=p

The average of all possible sample proportions equals the true population proportion.

STANDARD ERROR

SE=σp^​​=np(1−p)​​

How much p-hat typically moves from sample to sample. Larger n makes a smaller SE.

LIKE 7.1, BUT FOR A SHARE

Means used σ/n​.

Proportions use p(1−p)/n​.

p-hat is successes divided by n. It is a share, not a count.

Say mean first: mu of p-hat equals p. The pile of sample proportions is centered at the true share. Then SE: square root of p times (1 − p) over n. That is the typical spread of those sample proportions. Compare once to 7.1: means divide sigma by square root of n. Proportions put p(1 − p) under the square root with n. Misconception: writing the SE as p(1 − p)/n without the square root. Another: calling the SE the mean.

When the normal model is OK

Success–failure: both at least 10

Expected successes

np ≥ 10

n times the true proportion p.

Expected failures

n(1 − p) ≥ 10

n times the failure share.

Then use

a normal curve

Mean p. SE = √[p(1 − p)/n].

If either count is under 10, do not treat p-hat as approximately normal for these probability questions.

Section 8.3 uses a related check for confidence intervals (successes and failures both more than 5). On Exam 2 sampling-distribution items, use np and n(1 − p) both ≥ 10.

Say success–failure before any probability. Expected successes: n times p. Expected failures: n times (1 − p). Both need to be at least 10. Then the sampling distribution of p-hat is approximately normal with mean p and that SE. Mention once that 8.3’s confidence-interval check is a cousin (more than 5 each). For these Exam 2 items, stick to ≥ 10. Misconception: checking only np and skipping failures.

Sample size for a proportion

Round n up. Worst case uses 0.5.

n=(Ez​)2p^​q^​

E is the desired margin of error. z matches the confidence level (90%: 1.645, 95%: 1.96). q^​=1−p^​.

No prior estimate? Use p^​=q^​=0.5. That choice makes n as large as it needs to be.

EXAM STYLE · 90%, WITHIN 0.04

n=(1.645/0.04)2(0.5)(0.5)=422.816

Round up → n = 423

DESMOS ↗  (1.645/0.04)^2*0.25  422.816… then always round up

Write the formula once. z over E, squared, times p-hat q-hat. No prior p-hat means use 0.5 and 0.5, so the product is 0.25. Example: 90% confidence, within 0.04. (1.645/0.04)^2 × 0.25 ≈ 422.816. Always round up, so n = 423. Never round to 422. A second flash if time: 95%, within 0.05 → (1.96/0.05)^2 × 0.25 = 384.16 → n = 385. Misconception: rounding down, or forgetting the 0.25 when there is no prior estimate.

Quick reminder from 8.1 notes

Sample size for a mean

n=(zEσ​)2

Known population standard deviation σ. Desired margin of error E. Same z cutoffs. Round up.

Proportion

n = (z/E)² p̂q̂

No prior? Use 0.5 × 0.5.

Mean

n = (z σ / E)²

Needs σ from the problem.

Both

Round up
