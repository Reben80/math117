MATH 117   Unit 4 · The normal distribution

Probability is an area

The shaded area under the curve is the probability.
The whole curve has area 1, which is 100%.

SECTION 6.2

Prof. Rebin Muhammad

Montgomery College

Say: a probability is an area under the curve. Run your hand over the shade and call that the probability a value lands there. The whole curve has area 1, which is 100%. Half the bell is 0.50, or 50%. A probability is never bigger than 1. Standard deviation is the spread, the step from chapter 6.1. Use the word standard deviation first. cdf starts with a score and returns an area. inversecdf starts with an area and returns a score. Less-than and less-than-or-equal are the same area on a smooth curve. Say that only if someone asks. About 30 minutes. If you are short, stop after the golf scores. The phone ages can wait.

Example 6.7 · read a decimal as a percent

The two areas add to 100%

\(P(X > a) = 1 - P(X \le a)\)

The shade is 0.0228, or 2.28%. The rest is 1 − 0.0228 = 0.9772, or 97.72%.

Point at the shade: that small left piece is 0.0228, which is 2.28%. The unshaded rest of the curve is the complement, 1 − 0.0228 = 0.9772. Have them add the two decimals and get 1, the whole area. The line is z = −2. The book does not name an x for this picture. Write P(X > a) = 1 − P(X ≤ a) once. On a smooth curve, less-than and less-than-or-equal have the same area. Try It 6.7, only if asked: 0.012 is 1.2% on the left, so the right area is 0.988, or 98.8%.

Example 6.8 · final exams, mean 63, sd 5

The probability of a score over 65

\(P(X > 65) = 1 - P(X \le 65) = 1 - 0.6554 = 0.3446\). About 34%. This is a probability. 69.4 and 65.6, later, are scores.

DESMOS  normaldist(63, 5)  then Right from 65

The shade is the right tail, the probability of a score over 65. 65 is only 0.4 standard deviations above 63, so the area is not small. The area to the left is 0.6554. Subtract from 1: 0.3446, about 34 out of 100. In Desmos, type only normaldist(63, 5), then choose Right from 65. Choosing Left and forgetting to subtract gives 0.6554, the wrong side. 69.4 and 65.6 are not this answer. Those are percentiles, scores, on the later slide.

Two different questions

Start with a score, or start with an area

cdf: given a score, find the area to its left. inversecdf: given an area, such as 90%, find the score.

Left graph: you are given the score. The shade is the unknown area. That is cdf. In Desmos, type normaldist with the mean and standard deviation, then choose the region. Right graph: you are given 90% and the score is the unknown edge. That is inversecdf. These two pictures use no book data. They are only the two questions. Misconception: using inversecdf when the problem already gives the score, or using the area box when the problem asks for a cutoff.

Example 6.8 · the same exams

Finding a score from a percentile

The 90th percentile means 90% of the scores are at or below 69.4. The 70th percentile is 65.6. These are scores, not the probability of a score over 65.

DESMOS  normaldist(63, 5).inversecdf(0.90)  gives the score 69.4

Point at the shade, then at the edge. The shade is the 90% you were given. The edge, 69.4, is the score you were asked for. That is the opposite of the previous probability. inversecdf(0.90) returns 69.4. The 70th percentile is inversecdf(0.70) = 65.6. One sentence in the book says 65.5. Use 65.6. If someone asks about a score over 85: that is a probability, about 1, because 85 is about four standard deviations above 63. It is not a percentile. Misconception: calling 69.4 a probability. Ask whether 69.4 is between 0 and 1. It is a score.

Example 6.9 · hours a home computer is used

Mean 2 hours, standard deviation 0.5

First the probability: the book’s area between 1.8 and 2.75 is 0.5886, about 59%. Then a cutoff: the first quartile is 1.66 hours, with 25% of households at or below it.

DESMOS  normaldist(2, 0.5)  then Inner, 1.8 to 2.75

DESMOS  normaldist(2, 0.5).inversecdf(0.25)  gives the quartile 1.66

Do the interval first. The shade between 1.8 and 2.75 is a probability, 0.5886, about 59 out of 100 households. Type normaldist(2, 0.5), then Inner from 1.8 to 2.75. The two hours go in the area box. The mean and standard deviation go inside normaldist. Only after that, the quartile. inversecdf(0.25) is a cutoff, 1.66 hours. A quarter of the households are at or below it. Try It 6.9, only if asked: golf again, between 66 and 70, area 0.4950. Misconception: typing the hours 2 and 0.5 as the interval.

Example 6.11 · smartphone users, ages 13 to 55+

Mean age 36.9, standard deviation 13.9

The shaded half runs from Q1 = 27.52 to Q3 = 46.28. The IQR is 18.8 years. Forty percent are 40.4 or older, so 60% are to the left. Use inversecdf(0.60).

DESMOS  normaldist(36.9, 13.9).inversecdf(0.60)  gives about 40.4

This slide can wait if the hour is gone. The shade is the middle half, from the first quartile to the third. The book prints Q1 = 27.5246 and Q3 = 46.2754. The graph rounds those to 27.52 and 46.28. The difference is 18.75, and the book rounds the IQR to 18.8 years. The 40.4 question is separate. Forty percent are at least 40.4, so the area to the left of that age is 60%. inversecdf(0.60) = 40.4215, and the book rounds to 40.4. The trap is typing 0.40. That returns the age with 40% younger, the wrong side. Example 6.10, only if asked: between 23 and 64.7 is 0.8186, at most 50.8 is 0.8413, the 80th percentile is 48.6.
